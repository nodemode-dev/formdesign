# Harbour Trust Annual Review

![The restored slipway at low tide](trust-1.jpg)

## A letter from the chair

This was the year the harbour came back to life. The slipway that had been closed for a decade reopened in June, the boatyard welcomed its first school groups in September, and by the winter storms we had a waiting list for the apprentice programme for the first time in our history.

None of it would have happened without the volunteers who gave more than eleven thousand hours, the families who joined as members, and the local businesses who lent us tools, timber and their weekends. Thank you, all of you.

## The year in numbers

Membership grew by more than a third, and visits to the boatyard doubled once the new viewing deck opened. Income from the café and the shop now covers the running costs of the visitor centre, which lets every grant go straight to restoration work.

| Measure | Last year | This year |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Members | 1,240 | 1,690 |
| Boatyard visitors | 8,300 | 17,450 |
| Volunteer hours | 7,900 | 11,200 |
| Apprentices in training | 4 | 9 |
| Boats restored | 2 | 5 |

## Restoration

The largest project of the year was the slipway itself. Divers surveyed the old timbers in the spring, and a team of six rebuilt the cradle and rails over the summer using larch from the estate across the bay. The work came in on time and slightly under budget.

Five boats left the yard restored, including the pilot cutter Wren, which returned to the water in August after four years on the hard. She now takes visitors around the harbour on calm weekends.

![Apprentices fitting new planks](trust-2.jpg)

## Learning

The apprentice programme doubled in size. Nine young people are now learning traditional boatbuilding alongside modern repair methods, and two of last year's apprentices have taken jobs in yards along the coast.

Our school programme reached every primary school in the district. Children spent a morning in the yard, learning knots, measuring timber and hearing the stories of the boats that were built here a century ago.

- 42 school visits, up from 15
- 6 evening classes for adults
- 3 open days with demonstrations
- 1 new partnership with the regional college

## Looking ahead

Next year we will restore the net loft above the boatyard and open it as a workshop and exhibition space. We will also begin a survey of the harbour wall, the last major structure still waiting for repair.

We are grateful for every member, volunteer and supporter who has brought us this far. The harbour belongs to all of us, and there is still plenty of work to do.
